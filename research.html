Compound precipitation and wind extremes

Master's thesis · ongoing

My master's thesis investigates the long-term variability of compound precipitation and wind extremes in Europe using the LAERTES-EU regional climate model ensemble. I am interested in when these hazards occur together, how their characteristics vary across regions, and how the analysis depends on the definition of an extreme event.

I develop Python and Jupyter workflows to identify events, calculate precipitation and wind severity indices, and examine the spatial overlap of threshold exceedances. The work includes LAERTES ensemble members and ERA5 reanalysis, with attention to the different interpretation of model simulations and reanalysis.

Questions guiding the work

  • Where and how often do extreme precipitation and strong winds occur together?
  • How do seasonal choices, wet-day definitions, and percentile thresholds affect the results?
  • How do regional severity indices relate to the area experiencing both hazards?

Current analysis uses configurable thresholds, land masks, event rankings, frequency maps, and statistical plots. These workflows support an ongoing investigation of compound-event variability and severity.

Aerosols, cloud glaciation and lightning

Student research assistant · Cloud Physics

At KIT's Institute of Meteorology and Climate Research, Troposphere Research, I investigate relationships among lightning activity, aerosols, and cloud glaciation. The work combines global lightning observations from FLASHMAP, CAMS aerosol fields, and atmospheric information from ERA5.

My analysis brings these sources onto compatible grids and examines regional and global statistical patterns. It includes data alignment, spatial masks, nonlinear fitting, and comparisons across land, ocean, and continental regions.

Careful interpretation matters: aerosol fields provide proxies for aspects of the aerosol environment, and statistical relationships alone do not establish causation. I work with these distinctions when interpreting the connections between aerosols, cloud microphysics, and lightning.
